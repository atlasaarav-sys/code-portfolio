The quick engineer debugged the circuit board late into the night. The
circuit board hummed quietly on the workbench while the oscilloscope traced
a clean square wave. The engineer smiled and reached for another cup of
coffee. Outside, the rain traced quiet patterns on the window while the lab
computer compiled the firmware one more time. The firmware finally passed
every test and the engineer saved the project before heading home. The
project had taken three weeks longer than planned, but the final build was
stable and the telemetry pipeline reported clean data from every sensor.
The team celebrated with pizza and a short demo the next morning. The demo
went smoothly and the client asked thoughtful questions about the sensor
fusion algorithm. The algorithm combined readings from the accelerometer
and the gyroscope to estimate orientation in real time. Real time systems
demand careful attention to timing, and the engineer had learned that
lesson the hard way during an earlier project. The earlier project taught
the whole team to write better tests before shipping new firmware.
